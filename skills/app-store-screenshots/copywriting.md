# Copywriting: what goes on each screenshot

The words decide whether people install. Write and approve the copy **before** designing anything.

---

## 1. How shoppers actually see screenshots

- In App Store search results, people see only the **first 1–3 screenshots** (3 portrait on iPhone, 2 on iPad) before opening the page ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)).
- Most visitors never scroll past screenshot 3. Screenshots 4–10 are only seen by people who are already interested.
- You get about **1.5 seconds** per screenshot, and it is read at **thumbnail size**.

**So:** screenshots 1–3 must sell the app on their own. Everything after that is extra.

## 3. The job of each screenshot

**Screenshot 1: Hook (what is this, and why should I care?)**
- The single biggest promise or outcome of the app.
- 3–5 words. Use the main keyword people search for.
- Show the app's best screen or the emotional moment (a person, a result, a mascot).
- Add proof here if it is strong: rating, user count, press, award.
- Never a login, splash or empty screen (Apple guideline 2.3.3).

**Screenshot 2: Explain (what does it do?)**
- The core feature, shown in the real UI.
- 4–8 words. Name the feature in words people search for.

**Screenshot 3: Prove, or the second-best benefit**
- If the proof is real and strong (rating 4.5+ with many reviews, a large user count, press, an editorial feature): show it here.
- If the proof is weak: use a second benefit, and move proof later.

**Screenshots 4 and on: one benefit per screenshot, strongest first**
- Each covers one benefit only: a different feature, use case or outcome.

**Last screenshot: trust or close**
- Privacy, security, support, "works with your tools", or a final emotional line.

Never put two ideas on one screenshot.

---

## 4. Headline rules

1. **Benefit, not feature.** "Track your workouts effortlessly", not "Workout tracker". "Get emails that matter", not "Email filter".
2. **Short.** Screenshot 1: 3–5 words. Others: up to 8 words. If a headline needs 3 lines at thumbnail size, cut it.
3. **Plain words people would search.** No jargon or internal feature names.
4. **Specific beats vague.** Numbers, times and results: "15-min workouts", "2.7x faster", "in 30 sec".
5. **Talk to one person.** "you" / "your".
6. **One emphasis per headline.** Colour, highlight, weight or style on one word or line, never several.
7. **Readable at thumbnail size.** High contrast, big type. Text should cover no more than about 20% of the screenshot area (Google Play recommendation).
8. **The small line under the headline** (optional) adds the "how" or the proof in 3–8 words.

---

## 5. Headline formulas (used across the 18 styles)

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| Outcome promise | "Speak Spanish fast" · "Finally see results" |
| Verb + benefit | "Find the perfect stay" · "Keep your favorites" |
| Your + noun | "Your tanning buddy" · "Your money, everywhere" |
| X made Y | "Inventory tracking made effortless" |
| Number / time claim | "15-min workouts" · "Become fluent 2.7x faster" |
| Equation | "15 min = 1 hour" |
| Contrast / without | "Eat what you love, anywhere" |
| Question | "Why SPF 50 and not 30?" (in a chat bubble) |
| Identity | "#1 AI note taker" (only if true and provable) |
| Customer quote | "This completely changed how I create." |
| Social action | "Share with friends and collabs" |
| Trust | "Built for privacy" · "Family: in sync" |

---

## 6. Proof elements

Use only **real** numbers the user gives you. Never invent ratings, reviews, user counts or press.

- Star rating with laurels: "4.9 ★ · 9k+ ratings"
- User count: "1M+ users", "Join 300K+ learners"
- Press: "Featured on [publication]" (logo with permission)
- Awards and editorial picks
- Customer quote with first name
- Partner or integration logos ("Works with …")

Ask the user which of these they have.

---

## 7. Store rules the copy must follow

**Apple App Store** ([Review Guidelines 2.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- Screenshots must show the app in use (2.3.3).
- No prices or discounts (2.3.7, 2.3.14).
- No unverifiable claims (2.3.1, 2.3.14).
- No names or icons of other mobile platforms or app stores (2.3.10).
- Third-party logos and trademarks only with permission (5.2.1).
- Use fictional data, not real people's data (2.3.9).
- Content suitable for all ages (2.3.8).

**Google Play** ([preview asset rules](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151)), stricter:
- Show the actual in-app experience. Google recommends no device frames and no text or backgrounds that aren't part of the app UI. Many listings still use captions, but the clean variant is safest.
- Avoid "Best", "#1", "Top", "Million downloads", ratings, awards, testimonials and prices.
- Avoid calls to action ("Download now", "Try now").
- If you use taglines, keep them to about 20% of the image or less.
- No people handling the device, no store badges.
- Every screenshot needs **alt text** of 140 characters or less: write it with the storyboard.
- Up to 8 screenshots per device type.

If the user ships to both stores, make one Apple set and one Play-safe set: same design, adjusted claims.

---

## 8. Localisation

Translate headlines for each market (Apple recommends it). Don't just swap words; re-check the length so the text still fits, and adapt idioms.

---

## 9. Before showing the storyboard to the user, check

- [ ] Screenshot 1 says what the app is **and** why it matters, in 5 words or fewer.
- [ ] Screenshots 1–3 alone would convince someone to install.
- [ ] Every headline is a benefit, and every screenshot has one idea.
- [ ] Every number and claim is real (from the user).
- [ ] No prices; follows the store rules above.
- [ ] For Play: claims removed, alt text written (140 characters or less each).
- [ ] Every headline is readable at thumbnail size.
